## Title: Instrumentation Baseline Requirements for ISRU-oriented Lunar Mineral Mapping

## Abstract:
As the primary source of Fe and Ti for additive manufacturing and structural sintering as well as a source of OH obtained during Fe and Ti extraction utilised directly as propellant, Ilmenite (FeTiO) - an oxide, is the most versatile lunar resource. Ilmenite’s crystallographic structure traps He from the solar wind, positioning it as an indispensable resource for fusion energy applications. Beyond ilmenite, crucial are sulfide minerals as the principal carriers of chalcophile metals Ag, Cu, Au, Pb, Zn for electronics manufacturing and minerals that host siderophile metals including Ti, Fe, V, Cr, Mn, Pt, Pd. Critical for structural applications and oxide industrial catalysis. 

While each resource carries a distinct spectral signature, reliable identification from orbit faces interference specific to each range. In the VNIR range sulfide minerals are interfered with by pyroxenes, while detection of oxide materials becomes challenging due to primarily silicate-based, surrounding regolith. In the FIR range oxides and sulfides have strong absorption bands at 34.2 m and 38.8 m, without interference by each other, silicates or carbonates, but their detection might be blocked by host rock dilution and akaganeite, arsenides, microcline, prehnite if confirmed to be present on lunar surface. Ice/hydrothermal minerals signature on lunar surface and minerals host rock physical properties, such as grain size distribution or outcrop occurrence, produce no diagnostic signal in either VNIR or FIR, requiring a dedicated mid-IR channel for their characterization. 

As of 2026, the primary reference for wide lunar mapping were missions of 2008 that operated in the 0.4-3.0 m range with 70 m/pixel (M3) and 220 m/pixel (SIR-2) resolution. Although their results are still relevant, they were limited by low resolution, interference with oxide materials and ice in utilised VNIR and SWIR range. As a solution, a number of dedicated lunar prospecting missions are being proposed, with specification of payload becoming a rising debate. 

The paper defines detection baselines and minimum instrument specifications to map all priority ISRU minerals from a single satellite operating from near polar lunar orbit. To determine the optimal, minimal set of spectral bands across the broad 0.4–40 m range, this study evaluates multiple algorithmic approaches, including Integer Linear Programming and heuristic optimization. The proposed band subsets are rigorously scored using information-theoretic metrics—prioritizing maximum mutual information and high spectral separability to ensure target ISRU resources can be confidently distinguished from interfering non-target minerals. Results of the study are then compared to planned near future lunar mapping missions, accessing their planned methodology and feasibility.
